An **alarm** detects a condition and holds it; an **action** does something about it. A simple
action is a single step (a `notify`); a multi-step action is a **flow**. Both are **stateful
entities that hold their state directly** (not event-sourced). An action's credentials live
in credentials; the Expr and Go-template machinery in [expressions](/architecture/expressions/).

:::note[Partial]
Built today: the **alarm as a held row**, raised on a **component** by a caller, carrying a `severity`
(a level of the [severity registry](#severity-a-registry-of-named-levels), by name), a message, a
`raised_at`, and a nullable `cleared_at` (clearing keeps the row). Any active alarm reaches a system
through the component's own verdict (any active alarm degrades it, one at or above the `high` level
is an outage), and such an alarm impairs every [role](/architecture/health/) that component occupies
while it stays open, since only an outage
component stops occupying its slots; a lesser alarm degrades the component but leaves it in place
(#626: an alarm no longer names what it degrades).

Also built: **acknowledgement** ([below](#acknowledgement-a-fact-about-a-person)), the record that a
human has seen an incident, orthogonal to whether it is still raised. **Snooze** and **resolve** are
not built, and the reasons are in that section.
See [implementation status](/architecture/status/).
:::

## The alarm (a stateful entity)

**Metaphor: a morning alarm, not a pop-up.** A pop-up *alert* appears and is gone (a `notify`
action); an **alarm** goes off when its condition is met and **stays high until interacted with**.
The `alarm` row **holds its current state directly** (`status`, `opened_at`, `resolved_at` in the
target design; built today: `severity`, `message`, `raised_at`, `cleared_at`, `acknowledged_at`,
`acknowledged_by`).

:::design[Target design (ADR-0050)]
**One incident, a new row per open**, keyed by `(event_rule, owner)` (the exclusive-arc owner, so a
system- or location-owned sample yields a system/location-owned alarm), the ITSM correlation anchor
([samples](/architecture/properties/)). The open and resolve **events** carry the `alarm_id` and are
the edge log; the alarm row is the live state, never reconstructed from them.

**PG-first**: the firing `event_rule` consumer writes the alarm row in the **same Postgres
transaction** as the event, serialized per `(event_rule, owner)` so an incident never double-opens;
a [CDC publisher](/architecture/messaging/) fans the committed transition onto JetStream for
`action_rule` consumers. No dual-write; Postgres is never a message bus.

**opened / resolved** are rule-driven (`fire_criteria` / `clear_criteria`), each emitting an `event`
with the `alarm_id`; the operator-driven transitions are recorded in `audit_log` (also carrying the
`alarm_id`) and applied to the row. The timeline assembles by `alarm_id` across events + audit. The
acknowledgement half of that is built and works exactly this way ([below](#acknowledgement-a-fact-about-a-person));
snooze is not.
:::

An alarm write is **not sample-silent**: raising or clearing one recomputes
[health](/architecture/health/) in the same transaction, and the rollup records the verdict
transition as a **calculated-provenance `property` sample** (`provenance='calculated'`,
`source_rule='health-rollup'`). Cycle safety therefore rests on **provenance, not topology**
([ADR-0069](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0069-cycle-safety-is-provenance-based-not-topology-based),
see *Cycle safety*).

## Acknowledgement: a fact about a person

An alarm's **raised** state is a fact about its **condition**: the one-open invariant is per
`(component, dedup_key)` ([ADR-0075](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0075-an-alarm-carries-its-condition-identity)),
and it is the condition, not a person, that decides whether the alarm is still up. An
**acknowledgement** is a fact about a **person**: somebody has seen this. The two are therefore
**orthogonal**, stored as two nullable columns rather than one `status` enum, and an alarm sits in
any of these states:

| | unacknowledged | acknowledged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **raised** | nobody has looked; the queue an operator works | somebody is on it, and it is still broken |
| **cleared** | it came and went unattended | it was seen, then fixed |

`POST /components/{name}/alarms/{id}:acknowledge` records it, gated by **`alarm:acknowledge`**
(held by `operator` and above). Three properties follow from acknowledgement being a fact about a
person:

- **It does not recompute health.** Acknowledging is not fixing. A recompute here would record a
  verdict transition at a moment when nothing about the fleet changed, and the health history is
  meant to be edges and only edges.
- **It carries its own permission and its own scope**, resolved on the component tier from
  `alarm:acknowledge` itself rather than from `component:update`. Recording that somebody looked is
  not editing the component, and a responder role may hold one without the other
  ([identity and access](/architecture/identity-access/)).
- **It is idempotent.** The first sighting is the one that matters operationally (time to
  acknowledge), so a second acknowledgement keeps the first person and the first time and writes no
  second audit row, the same shape as a re-raise of an open condition returning the existing
  incident.

**Un-acknowledging is not a verb.** If it turns out to matter, it is a second verb and a second
decision, not a nullable field somebody flips
([#728](https://github.com/hyperscaleav/omniglass/issues/728)).

:::caution[Snooze and resolve are deliberately absent]
Both are commonly bundled with acknowledge, and neither is built, for reasons rather than sequencing:

- **Snooze** exists to suppress **notification**. The outbound notification registry is unbuilt
  (#618), so a snooze today would suppress nothing: a column that lies. It belongs with #618 or
  after it.
- **Resolve** is either the existing `DELETE /components/{name}/alarms/{id}` (clear) under a second
  name, or a different concept nobody has specified. A human "resolving" an alarm whose raised state
  belongs to its condition is a contradiction until somebody says what it means.

A three-verb permission string bundling acknowledge with both of them appeared for most of a year in
this repo's test fixtures and in an earlier draft of the
[identity and access](/architecture/identity-access/) page. It was never a design: nothing seeded it
and no route ever enforced it
([ADR-0109](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0109-an-alarm-carries-an-acknowledgement-and-not-a-snooze-or-a-resolve)).
The permission lint now catches any attempt to write it back, because `alarm` is a resource the
route surface knows and neither of the other two verbs gates anything.
:::

## The `event_rule`

An `event_rule` raises alarms on its own (#918, #919; [ADR-0144](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0144-alarms-are-raised-by-event-rules-at-a-severity-level-from-a-ranked-registry)).
It reads one **sample key** (a registered property or metric, whose lane it records), carries a
required **`fire_criteria`** and an optional **`clear_criteria`**, and names a **severity level**.
When a landed sample meets the fire criteria the rule opens an alarm on the sample's component; when
one meets the clear criteria it clears it; a rule with no clear criteria clears on the **negation** of
its fire criteria. There is no separate `alarm_rule`.

```yaml
event_rule:
  name: reach-lost
  sample_key: endpoint-reachable       # a property (text) or a metric (number)
  fire_criteria: { op: eq, value: down }
  clear_criteria: { op: eq, value: up } # optional: absent, the negation of fire
  fire_sustain: 30s                     # how long fire must hold
  clear_sustain: 0s
  severity: high
  selector: { component_types: [display], components: [] }   # empty: every component
```

The evaluator judges each sample **as it lands, in the same transaction**, through the same guarded
conditional insert, audit row and health recompute a hand-raised alarm uses, so a rule-raised alarm
feeds health exactly as a hand-raised one does; it writes as the system actor. The alarm names its rule
(`raised_by_rule_id`, set null if the rule is deleted), and its condition identity is the rule's name
plus the sample's **instance**, so a per-endpoint input holds **one alarm per endpoint**: a component
whose SNMP and HTTP both fail carries two, and one recovering clears only its own. A repeated or
redelivered sample changes nothing. A disabled rule evaluates nothing, and re-enabling it opens no
duplicate against an alarm still open.

**Sustains** hold an edge until it has lasted: `fire_sustain` before the alarm opens and
`clear_sustain` before it clears, both defaulting to `0s`, set independently (a long clear sustain
over a short fire sustain holds an incident through a noisy recovery without delaying the page). A
sample pointing at an action starts or continues a **streak** (stored per rule, component and instance,
so it survives a restart); a sample that points nowhere (neither criterion holds), or at an action
already in force (a fire with the alarm open, a clear with none), breaks it, as does a sample the rule
cannot evaluate; and once it has held for the sustain the action takes effect, either on a later sample
or on the server's **sweep** (every five seconds, beside the command settlement sweep), which is what
lets a sustain work on an input that reports only on change, like `endpoint-reachable`. Before acting,
the sweep re-judges the series' newest sample and checks the rule still selects the component, and it
serializes with an arriving sample on the same series, so a recovery landing as the sustain runs out
never leaves the alarm open. Sustains are timed by the **server's receipt** of each sample, never by the
sample's own timestamp, so a node whose clock runs behind neither skips nor stretches a sustain (which
sample is newest is still decided by that timestamp). Only a **material** edit restarts a rule's
streaks (its key, criteria, clear criteria, selector or sustains); a label, description, message or
severity edit leaves them running. Creating a rule, enabling one, or a material edit **primes** it from
each selected series' newest sample, so a condition that already holds starts its sustain then (or
fires at once with no sustain) instead of waiting for a change the input may never send. The platform ships one official rule, **`reachability-lost`**: an endpoint
whose verdict reads `down` for 15 seconds opens a `high` alarm on its component, and reading `up` clears
it, so unplugging a simulated component takes its system out and plugging it back recovers it, with no
operator in between. That takes the system out only when the component fills a role whose impact
is `outage` and losing it drops the role below quorum; otherwise the system reads whatever the role's
impact says, and a component that fills no role moves no system.

Criteria are, for now, one comparison of the arriving sample's value, `{op, value}`: a property
compares text (`eq`, `ne`), a metric compares numbers (`eq` `ne` `gt` `ge` `lt` `le`); a sample of
the wrong type, or a number that is no number, decides nothing. The column is jsonb, so the
[expression](/architecture/expressions/) leaf replaces the comparison without a schema change (#524).
The **selector** picks the components a rule watches: those whose type falls within a listed component
type, and those listed by id; empty, every component. A rule outlives what it points at: deleting a
component or a key it reads leaves it editable, and it simply matches nothing.

Rules are **fleet-wide config**: authored at `/event-rules` with `rule:*` and an all-scope grant, read
by any all-scope reader; a version bumps on every change.

A rule does not declare a health verdict itself: the alarm it raises impairs its owner's own
verdict (any active alarm degrades it, one at or above `high` is an outage). Only an outage owner stops
occupying the [system roles](/architecture/core-entities/#system-roles-the-slots-a-system-needs-filled)
it fills; a lesser alarm degrades it but leaves it in place. When it does drop out (#626), how much
that matters is the role's **impact** ([health](/architecture/health/)).

:::design[The rest of the event rule (ADR-0050)]
Still design: **expression criteria** and an **expression selector** (`scope:
'component.template == "polaris-dsp-16"'`, fan-out, one alarm per match) once the engine lands (#524);
a **window** (`window: { reduce: avg, over: 10m }`) aggregating the input before the criteria; a rule
**suppressible by name through the cascade** ([cascade](/architecture/cascade/)), so a high-weight group
removes a false-firing rule without editing it; and a rule scoped to whichever arc owns its sample,
yielding **system-level** alarms with the same machinery (a system-scoped rule reads member data and
fires a system-owned alarm).
:::


## Severity: a registry of named levels

Severity is a **registry of named levels**, not a bare integer. It takes the ordinary identity
triad, so it adds no fourth vocabulary: a **`name`** on the entity name rule, an optional
**`label`**, and its uuid **`id`** like any other row, plus a **`color`** and an integer
**`order`** used only for comparison. Rules reference a level **by name**; official defaults ship
**spaced**, so a new level inserts between two others without renumbering:

| name | label | color | order |
|---|---|---|---|
| info | Info | gray | 10 |
| warning | Warning | yellow | 20 |
| average | Average | orange | 30 |
| high | High | red | 40 |
| disaster | Disaster | dark red | 50 |

Severity is **distinct from health** ([health](/architecture/health/)); higher order is more severe.
The set is **operator-customizable** (relabel to P1/P2/P3, recolor, insert, three levels or seven),
all config. Predicates compare **by level** through the order (`alarm.severity >= "high"` matches
`high` and `disaster`); the UI renders the label and color.

Built (#917): the `severity_level` table (the order column is `rank`, since `order` is a reserved
word), the five official levels above from the boot seed, and operator levels at
`/severity-levels` (`omniglass severity-level create`, gated `severity_level:*`; a name or rank
already taken is a 409, an official level is read-only). An alarm's `severity` references a level
**by name** (a rename carries its alarms; a level an alarm names cannot be deleted). A component's
own verdict reads the rank: **`high` and above is an outage**, anything lower degrades. The retired
three values mapped by meaning: `critical` became `high`, `info` and `warning` kept their names.

:::caution[Open question]
Whether a severity level is purely a label, color, and order, or also carries policy such as a
default ack-timeout per level.
:::

:::design[The `action` entity (ADR-0050)]

## The action (a stateful entity)

What an `action_rule` raises and runs; stateful, holding its own state directly:

- **kinds**: `notify` (in-app), `webhook`, `email`, `run` (execute a command; edge realization in
  [templates](/architecture/templates/) / [nodes](/architecture/nodes/));
- a **simple** action carries delivery state (`queued / sent / failed / retried`, the at-least-once
  JetStream consumer);
- a **multistep** action is a **flow**, carrying workflow state (current step, waiting, branches).

The `action` row carries identity, kind, config, and current status.
:::

:::design[The `action_rule` (ADR-0050)]

## The `action_rule` (decoupled subscription)

Detection and response are **separate** (the `event_rule` does not contain its response, avoiding
Zabbix's action/operation tangle). An `action_rule` is a **NATS consumer** on the CDC-published
event / alarm stream, selecting with an Expr predicate, so one rule serves many alarms.
Subscriptions are **indexed by event key and label**, so dispatch evaluates only predicates whose
key or label matches; a rule may carry **multiple triggers** and fires if any matches (including a
label or wildcard trigger, e.g. any event labeled `room=boardroom-a`). It is a subscription, not a
fourth sample-pipeline rule family:

```yaml
action_rule:
  on: alarm
  when: 'transition == "open" && alarm.severity >= "high" && component.type == "device"'
  action: pagerduty-notify
```

The **source is polymorphic** but guarded (see *Cycle safety*): an alarm transition, a scheduled
fire (an `event` with `origin=scheduled`), an operator (manual), and the declarative runbook
step-list. Bodies are **Go templates**, sink auth a **credential reference**
([expressions](/architecture/expressions/)).
:::

:::design[Storm and dependency suppression (ADR-0050)]

## Storm and dependency suppression

The alarm grain stays **`(event_rule, owner)`**; two primitives keep the fan-out from becoming a
page storm. **Dependency suppression** mutes a child alarm whose owner's **parent entity on the
[exclusive-arc](/architecture/properties/) structural tree** is in an `outage` health state (open,
not dispatched); the arc gives the parent walk for free. **Action-level grouping** coalesces alarms
sharing **owner / label / `correlation_id`** into one **action dispatch** (one ticket with N
members) at the dispatch edge; the alarms stay distinct rows.

A **system-scoped `event_rule`** is the sanctioned upstream-cause dedup lever. Worked example: a
switch reboot downs 20 endpoints; the system-scoped rule owns the room-level cause as one
system-owned alarm, dependency suppression mutes the 20 child alarms, and grouping coalesces the
rest into one dispatch: the cause, not 20 symptoms.
:::

::::design[Action durability and egress (ADR-0050)]

## Durability and egress

Action state is **PG-first + CDC-out**: the step transition commits to the `action` row, the
leader-elected CDC publisher fans it onto JetStream, and the **external send** is a **JetStream
consumer** (retry with backoff, dead-letter), not a Postgres `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`
relay. Sends are **at-least-once**; sinks tolerate dupes or an **idempotency key** (alarm + action +
transition) makes the outcome exactly-once. Pipeline order: **render the body, then apply auth over
the rendered bytes** (HMAC signs the rendered body), then send. **Egress safety** is always on:
block internal / metadata IPs, verify TLS, bound timeouts, control redirects.

:::caution[Open question]
The dead-letter surface and the operator retry of failed actions.
:::

:::caution[Open question]
The observed-use auth-failure feedback from actions into credential health (paired with the
credential-health model in [config and credentials](/architecture/variables/)).
:::

:::design[Cycle safety in the action layer (ADR-0069)]

## Cycle safety in the action layer

The `collection -> sample -> alarm` core is acyclic by construction, and **only data authors
events**: an `event_rule` over samples (plus the clock's `origin=scheduled`) is the *only* way an
event enters the log; flows and actions never manufacture events. That leaves one possible loop, the
**data-mediated control loop** (an action commands a device, the new state arrives as a sample,
opens an alarm, fires the action again), closed with three rules:

1. **Consequence writes are provenance-labeled, and a rule never routes on its own consequences.**
   Every consequence write carries `provenance='calculated'` with its producing rule named (the
   health rollup's `property` row, `source_rule='health-rollup'`), and the routing layer refuses to
   trigger a rule off its own output
   ([ADR-0069](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0069-cycle-safety-is-provenance-based-not-topology-based)).
2. **Operator transitions do not match `action_rule`s** (only open / resolve do). Acknowledgement is
   the built one and behaves this way already: it fires nothing, because it recomputes nothing.
   Snooze remains unbuilt and refused (ADR-0109).
3. **The control loop is lineage-guarded at dispatch**: before running an action, the engine walks
   the triggering event's **causation lineage** (carried on **NATS message headers**); if the same
   `(action, owner)` appears upstream it is suppressed, with a depth bound as backstop. Flows are
   finite by construction (a step list, per-open-alarm, gated on open, cancelled on resolve or acknowledgement).

The walk crosses the device round trip because the command **stamps its `correlation_id` onto the
intended write and onto the adaptive-poll's observed sample**
([samples](/architecture/properties/)), and the `event_rule` firing off that sample inherits the id.
The carrier crosses the lane boundary as header (data lane) -> PG column -> header (record lane):
the `event_rule` writes the sample's `correlation_id` **and a `source_event_id` parent edge** onto
the `event` row ([events](/architecture/events/)), and the CDC publisher re-emits both.

### Correlation id: the trace of a causal chain

The same lineage powers a read-side **trace**: a **correlation id** threading the whole causal
chain (sample -> event -> alarm -> flow / action -> command -> the value change that clears the
alarm), stamped at the head and propagated along each caused edge on **NATS message headers**, so
an operator can query "everything this one event set in motion" (the `alarm_id` links only one
alarm's own open / clear events). **Not** a sample kind, **not** a stored span subsystem: no new
tables, no tracing backend.
:::

:::design[Flows (ADR-0050)]

## Flows: the multi-step action

A **flow** is a bounded multi-step action: a **DAG of steps** (`notify`, `command`, `wait`,
`branch`, `parallel`) over one alarm, **instantiated per open alarm**, gated on the alarm staying
open, **cancelled on resolve or ack**, **cycle-guarded** by the lineage walk above, **finite** by a
depth / step cap, its `wait` steps on the durable per-incident timer ([time](/architecture/time/)).

The canonical case is an **escalation**: remediate, `wait`, re-check the **real sample** the alarm
is built on, escalate if unchanged. The same engine covers a time-bound access grant and an
AI-troubleshooting flow. It is deliberately a **bounded** workflow engine, not Turing-complete; a
flow does **not** author events, and any effect it has returns as ordinary data (the only edge that
could re-open its alarm, lineage-bounded). A drag-and-drop editor edits flows. **Single-step and
multi-step are one model**: only the length of the step list distinguishes them.
:::

:::design[Registry namespacing for rules, actions, and severity levels (ADR-0050)]

## Namespacing

Event rules, actions, and severity levels carry the same **`official` boolean** discipline as the
rest of the registries, seeded through each registry's authoritative upsert (`UpsertPropertyType`
and siblings): `official: true` ships vetted, `official: false` is operator-authored and central to
component templates.
:::

## Storage

`alarm` and `action` are **stateful entities that hold their current state directly** (not
event-sourced); the physical layout (the owner arc, partitioning) lives on
[storage](/architecture/storage/).

The built `alarm` row is component-local: no owner arc and no `event_rule`. It **does** carry its
acknowledgement (#728), as two nullable columns rather than a `status` enum, because an enum would
force acknowledged and cleared into one column and make them mutually exclusive, which they are
not. The **target design shape** adds `event_rule`, the owner arc, `status`, `opened_at`, and
`resolved_at`.

:::design[The `action` entity (ADR-0050)]
`action` is a stateful entity carrying delivery and step state (`id`, ordered `steps` of
notify/command/wait/branch, `status`, `current_step`), driven by events and alarms. No `action`
table exists today.
:::

A command **is two real tables**: the `command_type` registry and the `command` invocation row with
its computed settlement; see [commands](/architecture/commands/). The `event_rule` / `action_rule`
config rows live with the [rule families](/architecture/calculations/).

## Model-keyed command cascade

An abstract action (`reboot`) resolves to different concrete commands by component type / model
through the cascade, so one `reboot` targets a heterogeneous fleet and each device runs the command
its model declares; the edge dispatch and the `command` declaration live in
[templates](/architecture/templates/) / [nodes](/architecture/nodes/).
