An **alarm** is a raised problem on a component, held from the moment it is raised until it clears. An
**event rule** raises and clears alarms on its own as samples land; a **severity level** says how bad an
alarm is and what it does to its component's [health](/architecture/health/); an **action rule** decides
when a **notification** about an alarm is sent, and to which targets. Detection and response are kept
apart: a rule that detects never contains its response.

To operate these, see [rules](/admin/rules/) and [notifications](/admin/notifications/) for the
admin, and [alarms](/operator/alarms/) and [notifications](/operator/notifications/) for the operator.

## The alarm (a stateful entity)

An alarm is a morning alarm, not a pop-up: it goes off and **stays raised until something clears it**.
The `alarm` row holds its state directly rather than being rebuilt from a log: a `severity` (a
[severity level](#severity-a-catalog-of-named-levels) by name), a `message`, `raised_at` and a nullable
`cleared_at`. Clearing sets `cleared_at` and keeps the row; clearing an alarm already cleared is an
explicit miss, not a silent success.

An alarm is raised by a caller (an operator or an API client, on a component) or by an
[event rule](#the-event_rule). Its identity is `(component, dedup_key)`
([ADR-0075](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0075-an-alarms-condition-identity-is-a-raiser-supplied-dedup-key)):
at most one alarm per identity is open at a time, enforced by a partial unique index, and a re-raise of
an open alarm returns the one already open. A hand-raised alarm's key defaults to its message; a key
beginning `rule:` is reserved for rules and refused (a 422).

Raising or clearing an alarm recomputes health in the same transaction, and the rollup records the
transition as a calculated `property` sample (`source_rule='health-rollup'`). An open alarm moves its
component by the effect its level declares; only an `outage` component stops filling its
[slots](/architecture/core-entities/#system-slots-what-a-system-needs-filled).

**An alarm's audit rows outlive it.** Every row other than the raise carries the field it moved in both
images (`cleared_at`, `acknowledged_at` or `snoozed_until`) and names the alarm, the component and the
dedup key, so the row says on its own what was done. Removing an alarm (deleting its component does
today, and retention will, #1339) never touches `audit_log`
([audit](/architecture/audit/),
[ADR-1442](/architecture/decisions/#adr-1442-an-audit-row-outlives-its-subject-both-images-and-a-string-reference)).

## The alarm's timeline

An alarm's history is one read (#950): `GET /components/{name}/alarms/{id}:timeline`
(`omniglass component alarm timeline <name> <id>`, gated `alarm:read`). It merges, oldest first:

- the **events that carry the alarm** (`event.alarm_id`): the rule's fire event that opened it and its
  clear event that resolved it;
- its **audit rows**: the raise, each acknowledgement, snooze and unsnooze, the clear, and a rule edit
  that turned a severity on or off, naming the editor;
- the **notifications** it caused, each after the edge that queued it, with its delivery status as it
  stands.

Each entry says when, which kind, what, and who: the person who acted, or the rule the entry belongs to.
At one instant an event sorts before the audit row it caused, since both share the transaction's clock.
The read is two bounded statements over the component the route already resolved, never a read per
entry; a component outside the caller's read scope is the same non-disclosing 404 an unknown one is. A
hand-raised alarm writes no events, so its timeline is its audit rows. In the console every alarm row
carries a clock button that opens its timeline under the row.

## Acknowledgement: a fact about a person

An alarm's **raised** state is a fact about the **fault**, decided by whoever raised it. An
**acknowledgement** is a fact about a **person**: somebody has seen this. They are orthogonal, stored as
two nullable columns rather than one status, so an alarm sits in any of four states:

| | unacknowledged | acknowledged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **raised** | nobody has looked: the queue an operator works | somebody is on it, and it is still broken |
| **cleared** | it came and went unattended | it was seen, then fixed |

`POST /components/{name}/alarms/{id}:acknowledge` records it, gated `alarm:acknowledge` (held by
`operator` and above, #728).

- **It does not recompute health.** Acknowledging is not fixing, and the health history is edges only.
- **It has its own permission and scope**, resolved on the component tier from `alarm:acknowledge`
  itself, not from `component:update`: a responder may hold one without the other.
- **It is idempotent.** The first sighting is the one that matters (time to acknowledge), so a second
  keeps the first person and time and writes no second audit row.

Un-acknowledging is not a verb; if it ever matters it is a second verb and a second decision.

**Resolve is deliberately absent.** It is either clear under another name, or a concept nobody has
specified: a person "resolving" an alarm whose raised state belongs to its rule is a contradiction until
somebody says what it means
([ADR-0109](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0109-an-alarm-carries-an-acknowledgement-and-not-a-snooze-or-a-resolve)).

## Snooze: silencing an alarm for a while

A **snooze** says: stop telling people about this alarm for a while (#1318). It sits on the alarm's
identity, `(component, dedup_key)`, not on one alarm row: by the time an operator sees a raise it has
already been sent, so a snooze on one row could only hold back its clear. An alarm that flaps opens a new
row on every re-raise, and one snooze covers each of them until its deadline
([ADR-1318](/architecture/decisions/#adr-1318-a-snooze-is-a-fact-about-an-alarms-condition-its-notifications-are-written-suppressed-and-its-end-tells-people-again)).

```bash
omniglass component alarm snooze display-1 <alarm-id> --duration 1h
omniglass component alarm unsnooze display-1 <alarm-id>
```

`:snooze` takes a `duration` from `1m` to `7d`, run from now; outside that bound is a 422, since longer
is a maintenance window or a paused rule. It may be set from any row of the alarm, cleared ones included
(silencing one that has just cleared is the usual case for a flapping alarm). Snoozing again replaces the
deadline; `:unsnooze` ends it early and is a 200 that writes nothing when nothing is snoozed. Both are
gated `alarm:snooze` (held by `operator`, not by `viewer`), scoped like acknowledgement, and audited on
the alarm they were called on, so they appear on its timeline. Every alarm read carries `snoozed_until`
and `snoozed_by` while it holds.

**What it holds back: every notification of the alarm.** While the alarm is snoozed, each row the action
rules would write for a raise or a clear is written with the terminal status **`suppressed`**, the reason
in `last_error`. Nothing is sent, nothing reaches an inbox, and the timeline keeps every suppressed row. A
row already queued and waiting out a retry is suppressed when its turn comes, so "stop telling me" also
stops the retry on its way. A suppressed row is never released or retried.

**When it ends, an alarm still raised is announced again**, as a plain `raised` transition judged against
the rules and memberships as they stand then; one that cleared during the snooze gets nothing. The held
rows are never replayed, so nobody gets a backlog of stale messages. The deadline sweep runs on the
notification tick (every 2 s) and takes each expired snooze `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, deleting it in the
transaction that queues its announcement, so two replicas never announce one alarm twice.

A snooze governs notifications and nothing else: it does not acknowledge, does not move health, is not
ended by a clear, and notifies nothing itself except at its end.

## The `event_rule`

An **event rule** watches one sample key on the components it selects, and **fires** when its criteria
hold and **clears** when they stop (#819, #918;
[ADR-0144](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0144-alarms-are-raised-by-event-rules-at-a-severity-level-from-a-ranked-registry)).
There is no separate alarm rule.

```yaml
event_rule:
  name: reach-lost
  sample_key: endpoint-reachable       # a property (text) or a metric (number)
  fire_criteria: { expr: 'value == "down"' }
  clear_criteria: { expr: 'value == "up"' } # optional: absent, it clears on !(fire)
  fire_sustain: 30s                     # how long fire must hold
  clear_sustain: 0s                     # how long clear must hold
  sample_window: 0s                     # a metric's trend rule: the history its criteria read
  severity: high                        # optional: absent, informational (no alarm)
  fire_event_type: rule-fired           # the event written when it fires
  clear_event_type: rule-cleared        # optional: the event written when it clears
  selector: { component_types: [display], components: [] }   # empty: every component
```

The **selector** picks components whose type falls within a listed component type (subtypes included)
and components listed by id; empty, every component. A rule outlives what it points at: deleting a
component or a key it reads leaves it editable, matching nothing.

### Firings, alarms and events

A rule is **firing** on a component and instance while it holds a row in `event_rule_firing`, whether
or not it raises an alarm; each such stretch is one **firing**. When a rule fires it writes the firing,
opens its alarm when it has a severity, and writes its **fire event**; when it clears it deletes the
firing, resolves the alarm, and writes its **clear event** if it names one
([ADR-0145](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0145-a-rule-records-its-conditions-as-typed-events-and-its-alarm-is-optional)).
Each happens once per edge, never per sample, in one transaction with the alarm transition and the health
recompute.

- **The alarm is optional.** A rule with no severity is informational and raises none.
- **The events are typed**, by name from the [event type](/architecture/events/) catalog: a required
  `fire_event_type` and an optional `clear_event_type`. The platform seeds a generic pair, `rule-fired`
  and `rule-cleared`; a rule may name a pair the catalog already models (`call-started`, `call-ended`).
  A type a rule names cannot be deleted.
- **The event is derived**: `origin = derived`, naming its rule in `derived_by_rule_id` and, in
  `source_rule`, the rule's name and version as they were when it was written. It carries the rule's
  message, the sample's key and value, and the alarm it opened or resolved in `event.alarm_id`.
- **Turning a severity on or off** opens or resolves alarms for the current firings and writes no event,
  since the firing did not move; the alarm's audit rows name the editor.

**A rule's alarm is identified by the rule's uuid and the sample's instance**: its dedup key is
`rule:<uuid>[:<instance>]`, so a per-endpoint input holds one alarm per endpoint, and one recovering
clears only its own. Every path that reads a rule's alarms matches on `raised_by_rule_id` plus that
instance (#977), so an alarm a replica from before #977 keyed by the rule's name still counts during a
rolling upgrade, and a key never matches another rule's alarm.

**A hand clear holds until the rule clears and fires again.** Firing is the firing row, not the alarm, so
an operator clearing a rule's alarm leaves the firing alone, and a further sample that still meets the
fire criteria does not reopen it. The next alarm opens on the next new edge, the same manual close a
Zabbix trigger allows.

**A rule's name is renameable; everything refers to its uuid**
([ADR-0076](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0076-a-renameable-human-typed-identifier-stays-in-the-url-and-the-write-returns-the-uuid)).
`POST /event-rules/{name}:rename` moves the name; alarms, firings, streaks, events and audit rows hold
the uuid, so nothing else moves and no version bumps. An official rule keeps its name (a 409), since the
boot seed matches it by name.

Disabling or deleting a rule clears its firings, each with its clear event, resolving their alarms;
narrowing its selector ends those on the components it no longer watches.

### Judged as samples land

The evaluator judges each sample **in the ingest transaction**, through the same guarded write, audit
row and health recompute a hand-raised alarm uses, acting as the system actor. A repeated or redelivered
sample changes nothing.

**Sustains** hold an edge until it has lasted: `fire_sustain` before firing, `clear_sustain` before
clearing, both defaulting to `0s` and set independently (a long clear sustain holds an alarm through a
noisy recovery without delaying the raise). A sample pointing at an action starts or continues a
**streak**, stored per rule, component and instance in `event_rule_state` so it survives a restart; a
sample pointing nowhere, at an action already taken, or that the rule cannot evaluate breaks it. Once a
streak has held for its sustain, the action takes effect on a later sample or on the server's **sweep**
(every 5 s), which is what lets a sustain work on an input that reports only on change. The sweep
re-judges the series' newest sample first. Sustains are timed by the **server's receipt** of each
sample, never the sample's own timestamp, so an outpost whose clock lags neither skips nor stretches one.

Only a **material** edit (key, criteria, selector, sustains) restarts a rule's streaks. Creating,
enabling or materially editing a rule **primes** it from each selected series' newest sample, so
criteria that already hold start their sustain at once instead of waiting for a change the input may
never send.

A component can also move into or out of a rule's selection without the rule changing, when it changes
type. The storage step for that (`reselectComponentRules`, #2475) primes the rules it came into and ends
the firings of the rules it left. No component, product or type write calls it yet (#941, #1920).

A **reconcile pass** (#928) heals the narrow races left: a rule enabled while a batch carrying its key is
uncommitted, or a judgement Postgres aborts on a deadlock. Once a minute, on a loop of its own, it
re-judges each enabled rule's newest sample on every series that took one since the pass before last
and corrects only what disagrees, each correction in its own transaction under the ingest's lock order.
Agreement, the common case, costs no write. It finds new samples by id through each day's block range
index, so a finished day costs a few index pages
([ADR-1929](/architecture/decisions/#adr-1929-a-finished-day-is-summarized-and-the-reconcile-keeps-its-id-marks)).

### Criteria are expressions

Criteria are [expressions](/architecture/expressions/) at the **criteria** site (#957), stored as
`{"expr": "..."}`, with four bindings: `value` (a metric's number, or a property's value typed by its
data type), `key`, `instance` (the sample's endpoint, empty when none) and `component` (its `name` and
`type`). A metric's criteria may also read `window` and `age`
([below](#a-trend-rule-reads-a-window-not-a-sample)). A criterion must yield true or false; one that
does not compile is refused at write (a 422 naming the criteria, line and column). A rule with no clear
criteria clears on `!(fire)`.

The short form `{op, value}` is still accepted on input and lowered to its expression (`ge 80` is
`value >= 80.0`); the API always answers with the expression. Criteria compile once per rule version. A
stored rule whose criteria no longer compile (a key's data type changed under them) holds the rule and
says so in `criteria_error`; a compile warning (`value == 21.5` on a measured number) rides in
`criteria_warnings` and never holds it. A sample the criteria cannot read, or a run over the site's
budget, holds the rule for that sample, logged once per rule version; the sample still lands
([ADR-0146](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0146-expressions-run-on-pinned-expr-with-an-allowlist-and-per-site-budgets-and-rule-criteria-are-expressions)).

**Preview** (`POST /event-rules:preview`, gated `rule:read`, #959) judges a candidate over the last 24
hours of its key's samples on the components the caller may read, with no sustains, and answers the
edges it would have written and where it would be firing now. It writes nothing, and caps its reads,
flagging `truncated` when it hits the cap. Criteria that read a window or an age are refused there,
since a preview judges each sample alone.

### Reading a rule

Rules read like a catalog: any `rule:read` grant lists and gets every rule. Writing one takes `rule:*`
on an all-scope grant, since a selector reaches every component. A version bumps on every change.

A rule's **live state** is a read, not a column: `GET /event-rules/{name}/state` (`omniglass event-rule
state list <rule>`) returns its firings, its open alarms and its streaks waiting out a sustain. The list
carries the same as counts (`firing`, `open_alarms`, `pending_streaks`), aggregated in one query, and
`last_judged_at`, which tells a quiet rule from one whose key has stopped arriving (#929): an ingest
batch stamps it at most once every 5 s, in its own `event_rule_live` row, never on the rule row. The
state names components, so it covers only those the caller's component read scope admits. The console's
**Catalog > Actions > Event rules** page lists every rule with its criteria in words, its severity and
those counts, and authors operator rules in a drawer; official rules are read-only.

### A trend rule reads a window, not a sample

A rule on a metric can judge where its series is heading (#1985). Its **`sample_window`** (up to a week)
binds the series' points over that window as **`window`**, oldest first, each a record of `value` and
`at` (unix seconds). The [trend functions](/architecture/expressions/#safety) fold it: `slope(window)`
is the least-squares rise per second, `span(window)` the seconds covered, and `growth(window)` the fitted
rise as a share of the series' mean. A leak reads:

```yaml
event_rule:
  name: process-heap-growing
  sample_key: process-heap-size
  sample_window: 6h
  fire_criteria: { expr: 'age >= 86400 && span(window) >= 18000 && growth(window) > 0.25' }
  clear_criteria: { expr: 'growth(window) <= 0.1' }
  fire_sustain: 1h
```

A fit, not the newest point less the oldest: noise on a flat series averages near zero, and one outlier
at either end does not read as a rise. The gap between fire and clear keeps a series on the line from
flapping.

Not every rise is a trend: a fresh database grows as its retention windows fill, and a restarted process
refills its caches for hours. So a metric's criteria also bind **`age`**, how long the series has run.
On a process's own runtime keys it is the process's uptime (a restart resets it); on any other key it is
the time since the series' oldest kept sample, so it never passes the metric lane's retention. The
process rules wait a day of uptime and `db-size-growing` a week. An age cannot tell bloat from a fleet
that grew, though, so `db-size-growing` **ships disabled**; an operator who wants it authors their own
rule from its criteria. The shipped-rule test walks each trend rule through the healthy growths it must
not fire on, and a rule ships enabled only if none fires it.

The store reads the window, not the expression, so the evaluator stays a pure function and a trend is
unit-testable on a fixed series. The window arrives as at most 360 even buckets, each the mean of its
newest four samples, so a read touches at most 1,440 rows a series. A `sample_window` its criteria never
read, or criteria reading `window` without one, is refused at write. The reconcile pass re-judges trend
series too, since a trend can move with no sample arriving, at most 200 a pass
([ADR-1985](/architecture/decisions/#adr-1985-a-trend-rule-reads-its-series-window-through-list-functions)).

:::design[The rest of the event rule, tracked in #1002 and #1023]
**System-owned alarms** (#1002): a rule scoped to whichever arc owns its sample, so a system-scoped rule
reads member data and raises a system-owned alarm with the same machinery. The alarm then gains the
owner arc, keyed `(event_rule, owner)`. Event rules do not judge a system-owned sample today.

**An expression selector** (#1023): `scope: 'component.product == "polaris-dsp-16"'`, fanning out one
alarm per match, beside the built type-and-id selector.

## Severity: a catalog of named levels

Severity is a catalog of named levels, not a bare integer. A level has the ordinary identity (a `name`,
an optional `label`, a uuid `id`), plus a `color`, a `rank` used only for comparison (higher is worse),
and a **`health_effect`**: what an open alarm at that level does to its component, `none`, `degraded`
or `outage` (#2558). Rules and alarms name a level by name; a rename carries its alarms, and a level an
alarm names cannot be deleted. The shipped levels, rendered from the seed:

<SeededSet kind="severity_levels" />

The official levels ship **spaced**, so an operator inserts a level between two without renumbering.
The set is operator-customizable (relabel to P1/P2/P3, recolor, insert, three levels or seven) at
`/severity-levels` (`omniglass severity-level create`), gated `severity_level:*` on an all-scope grant:
a level's health effect moves health fleet-wide, so a holder whose grant stops at one location reads
the levels and writes none (#917, #930). A name or rank already taken is a 409; official levels are
read-only. A custom level created without an effect takes the one its rank implies (below `warning`
none, below `high` degraded, else outage). Changing a level's effect recomputes every component with an
open alarm at it. The API's `outage` flag remains as a deprecated alias of `health_effect` being
`outage`.

The console reads the catalog wherever a severity shows: every alarm chip renders the level's label in
its color and sorts by its rank, so an operator level renders like a shipped one. **Catalog > Actions >
Severity levels** lists them worst first and authors operator levels in a drawer whose form shows where
a rank sits among the levels. An alarm whose level declares `none` says on its component that it does
not affect health.

Whether a severity level should carry more policy than its health effect, such as a default
acknowledgement timeout.
:::

## Notifications

A **notification** is one message about an alarm, sent on one channel to the people or tools somebody
already watches (#618). It is distinct from a [command](/architecture/commands/), sent to a component,
and from an [event](/architecture/events/), a record that is never sent. The lane is three catalogs and
a delivery row, each answering one question:

- a **notification type** says *what* a message says;
- a **notification target** says *where* it goes;
- an **action rule** says *when* it is sent;
- a **notification** is one message on its way.

### Notification types: what a message says

A `notification_type` carries a `label`, a `description`, the `official` flag, and two rules: a
one-line `subject` and a body `text`. The shipped types are authoritative on every boot and read-only;
an operator writes their own beside them and renames one with `:rename`.

<SeededSet kind="notification_types" />

Each rule is written in the [label rule](/architecture/decisions/#adr-2033-a-template-is-cloned-once-a-standard-is-built-to-and-the-label-string-is-a-rule) language and run by the same sandbox
(`internal/sandbox`): a closed grammar (`if`, comparisons and the label rule's functions; `printf`,
`call`, `index`, `range` and variables are refused at write, a 422 naming the construct) over a closed
map of strings, with the source, each call's arguments and each fact's length capped. The map is the
**alarm facts**, declared once beside the code that builds them; a fact outside it renders empty, and no
secret is in it:

<NotificationFacts />

`Link` is built on the `console_url` key of the `notification` [settings](/architecture/settings/)
namespace, since the server cannot know the name a receiver reaches it by.
`POST /notification-types/{name}:preview` renders a type, or a draft of either rule, against a real alarm
the caller can read, exactly as a delivery would carry it, and writes nothing. The console's
**Catalog > Actions > Notifications** page edits and previews through it; its neighbours **Action rules**
and **Notification targets** (#1346) author the other two catalogs.

### Notification targets: where a message goes

A `notification_target` names a **kind**, a destination and, optionally, the credential it proves itself
with. The kinds are a code-declared enum:

| kind | reaches |
|---|---|
| `webhook` | a URL: a chat incoming webhook, a paging service |
| `email` | a fixed set of addresses, a shared mailbox |
| `user_group` | every person in a group, on the channels named ([below](#people-group-and-person-targets-and-the-inbox)) |
| `user` | one person, on the channels named |
| `role` | every person holding a role ([below](#role-targets-and-the-shipped-platform-routing)) |

A target is **platform-owned and admin-only**: every route takes an all-scope `notification_target:*`
grant, and nothing below admin holds one. A webhook URL is itself a credential, so every read masks it to
its scheme and host, the audit log records it masked, and `:reveal` returns it whole under an audit row
of its own. The bound credential is a **platform secret** of type `hmac-key` (the send signs each body)
or `bearer-token` (the send presents it). Binding one takes `secret:bind` on it, and so does moving the
URL of a target that keeps one, since every send hands the credential to whatever the URL names
([ADR-2047](/architecture/decisions/#adr-2047-an-agent-may-do-what-its-person-may-except-take-a-credential-out-in-plaintext)).
A bound secret cannot be deleted from under its target. `POST /notification-targets/{name}:test` sends
one synthetic message (transition `test`, about no alarm), answers what the receiver said, and records
only its audit row.

**The send is render, then sign, then send.** The body is a fixed, versioned JSON envelope: the rules
render strings, never the JSON itself, so an operator's rule cannot produce an invalid body. The
signature covers the exact bytes sent and is the scheme the inbound `hmac` webhook verifies, so a
receiver can prove the sender, and Omniglass can verify its own notifications.

### People: group and person targets, and the inbox

A `user_group` or `user` target names people and the **channels** to reach them on: `inbox` (the
console inbox) and `email` (each person's directory email) (#1341). It rides a rule's targets exactly as
a webhook does:

```bash
omniglass notification-target create --name on-call-page --kind user_group \
  --user-group on-call --channels '["inbox"]'
omniglass action-rule create --name page-on-call --on '["raised"]' \
  --when 'severity_rank >= 50' --targets '["on-call-page"]'
```

- **A group expands at the transition**, in the alarm's own transaction, so a member added later is
  told on the next alarm and a removed one is not, with no rule edited.
- **Only people are told**: active, unarchived persons among the members; a machine user or an outpost
  in the group is skipped.
- **One row per person per channel**, each with its own delivery state; a person one rule reaches twice
  is told once.
- **Nobody is told about an alarm they could not read.** Each candidate is kept only when their own
  component read scope admits the alarm's component. The check is set-based, so it costs the same for a
  group of two or two hundred, and it runs under the notification savepoint, so it can never fail the
  transition
  ([ADR-1341](/architecture/decisions/#adr-1341-a-person-is-told-about-an-alarm-only-while-they-can-read-it-groups-expand-at-queue-time-scope-is-checked-there-and-again-at-delivery)).

**The inbox** is a person's own `inbox` rows, delivered by their own commit (written `sent`) with a
`read_at`. `GET /auth/me/notifications` lists the caller's entries newest first with the unread count;
`:markRead` and `:markAllRead` mark them, audited, and only the caller's own. Every inbox read is
scoped again by the caller's component read scope as it stands, so an entry about a component they
have since lost is gone.

A person-addressed row is visible wherever its alarm is, so the alarm's history stays whole, but its
recipient is named only to that person and to holders of `user:read:admin`
([identity and access](/architecture/identity-access/#people-as-notification-audiences)). Binding a group
or a person to a target takes `user_group:read:admin` or `user:read:admin` on top of
`notification_target:create`, and a group or person a target names cannot be deleted from under it.

### Role targets, and the shipped platform routing

A `role` target (#1987) reaches every person **holding a role**, directly or through a grant to one of
their groups, read from the grant table at the transition: grant someone `admin` and the next alarm
tells them. A person reached twice is told once, and the per-person scope check still decides which
alarms each holder hears about. Binding one takes `role:read:admin` and `user:read:admin`; a role a
target names cannot be deleted from under it.

**Platform alarms reach the admins by default.** The boot seed ships two targets and one rule, so a fresh
install tells somebody when Omniglass itself is unwell:

- `platform-admins`, a `role` target naming `admin`, on `inbox`;
- `platform-owners`, a `role` target naming `owner`, on `inbox` (owner does not inherit admin, and the
  bootstrap user holds `owner`);
- `platform-alarms-to-admins`, an action rule on `raised` and `cleared` with `when: platform`, sending to
  both.

`platform` is true when the alarm's component is one of Omniglass's own parts, a member of the reserved
[`platform` system](/operator/platform-health/). The three rows are **official**: the seed puts back
what it ships on every boot, and an edit or delete of those fields is a 409. What an operator owns
survives: disabling the rule or a target, a target's label, and extra targets added to the rule. The
shipped targets are inbox only; an email channel would fail a row per person on every install with no
mail relay set (#2016), so an operator who wants mail adds an email-channel role target of their own.

### Email: a shared mailbox, and a person's directory email

Email goes through the install's **mail relay** (#1317), set in the `notification` settings namespace:
`mail_relay` (host:port; empty turns email off), `mail_security` (`starttls`, the default; `tls`; or
`none`), `mail_from`, and `mail_secret`, a platform `basic-auth` secret. The relay is judged by the same
[egress contract](#durability-and-egress) a webhook is: an internal relay only when `egress_allow`
names it, and a credential never crosses an unencrypted session to anything but loopback. A settings
patch that binds or moves a `mail_secret` takes `secret:bind` on it.

An `email` target sends to its `addresses`. A person's email row sends to their **directory email**,
resolved at send, after the tick **judges the recipient again** in the row's own transaction: still an
active person, still in the group the row came through, still able to read the alarm's component. If
not, the row fails at once with the plain reason rather than mailing somebody who lost the alarm while
it waited out a retry. The message's `Message-ID` is the row's uuid, so every attempt carries the same
bytes.

**Opting out.** An admin may mark a platform rule `opt_out_allowed` (default false, so an on-call page
cannot be opted out of unless an admin says so). A member of a group it targets may then opt out with
`POST /action-rules/{name}:optOut` and back in with `:optIn` (`notification:subscribe`, #1354). A target
naming them directly still tells them, and turning `opt_out_allowed` off withdraws every opt-out.

### Personal action rules: subscribing yourself

A **personal action rule** (#1353) is an action rule with an owner (`owner_user_id`) and the channels it
tells its owner on, and no targets: its only recipient is its owner. It lives under
`/auth/me/action-rules`, gated `notification:subscribe`, which `viewer` carries, so everyone who can see
alarms can subscribe. Another user's rule is a 404 on every verb, and a person owns at most 50.

```bash
omniglass auth me action-rule create --name my-boardroom --on '["raised"]' \
  --when 'location == "Boardroom" && severity_rank >= 40' --channels '["inbox","email"]'
omniglass auth me action-rule test my-boardroom --when 'severity_rank >= 50'
```

It never matches outside its owner's scope: an alarm on a component the owner cannot read is skipped
before the rule's `when` runs, so neither a delivery nor the `when`'s own failure can tell them anything
about it. `:test` (and `:testDraft` for an unsaved draft) judges the rule against the owner's recent
alarms and says which it would have told them about, sending nothing.

### The `action_rule`: when a message is sent

An **action rule** wires an alarm transition to a notification: **on** one or both transitions
(`raised`, `cleared`), **when** a predicate over the alarm facts holds, **send** a notification type
(absent: the official one for the transition) **to** its targets. It is a rule, so it rides `rule:*`,
read like a catalog and written all-scope. Its `enabled` flag pauses it: a disabled rule queues nothing,
and what it queued before still delivers.

```bash
omniglass action-rule create --name page-ops --on '["raised","cleared"]' \
  --when 'severity_rank >= 40 && location == "Boardroom"' --targets '["ops-chat"]'
```

`when` is the **action** site of the [expression engine](/architecture/expressions/): its bindings are
the alarm facts in snake_case (`component`, `location`, `severity`, `message`, `rule`, ...), plus
`severity_rank` (so `severity_rank >= 40` is `high` and above) and `platform`. An empty `when` always
holds. Acknowledgement and snooze never notify, since an operator act is not an alarm's edge; a clear
is, whoever records it. One message per transition per rule per target: no grouping and no digest yet.

### Delivery: the `notification` row

A notification is a stateful row with its own delivery state: `queued`, `sent`, `failed`, or
`suppressed` (written while its alarm was [snoozed](#snooze-silencing-an-alarm-for-a-while), never
sent). It is written **in the same transaction as the alarm transition**, at the one seam every raise and
clear passes, so no transition is lost and none is written twice, and it is rendered then, so a retry
sends the same bytes and a preview is what a delivery carries.

A server tick (every 2 s) drains due rows, one per transaction under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, sending
while the row is locked, so two replicas never send one twice outside the crash window between a send
and its commit, and a restart loses nothing
([ADR-0618](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0618-notifications-are-postgres-rows-drained-by-a-server-tick-not-cdc-to-a-jetstream-sender)).
A failed attempt waits 10 s, doubling; the 8th failure is final (about 21 minutes in all). Every attempt
carries the same `Idempotency-Key`, the row's uuid, so a receiver drops a redelivery.

A transition never waits on this lane: the queue reads the rules, types and targets it matches
`FOR KEY SHARE SKIP LOCKED`, so a rule someone is deleting is skipped for that transition rather than
making the raise wait on a slow send (#1335).

A failed row that was attempted is re-queued with `POST /notifications/{id}:retry`
(`notification:retry`, an operator's), from the timeline or the CLI. `GET /notifications` lists rows by
alarm, target or status, and every read is scoped by the alarm's component, as the alarm reads are.

## Durability and egress

Delivery is **at-least-once** with an idempotency key, the pipeline is render, sign, send, and the
dead-letter surface is the `failed` row on the alarm's timeline with its `:retry`. All of it sits in
Postgres, drained by a tick, rather than on a JetStream consumer fed by change-data capture: a deliberate
divergence ([ADR-0618](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0618-notifications-are-postgres-rows-drained-by-a-server-tick-not-cdc-to-a-jetstream-sender)).

**Egress safety is one primitive**, `internal/egress`, the server's only outbound HTTP client, which every
sender (a webhook, the mail relay, the heartbeat, a document fetch) consumes rather than rewrites:

- A loopback, private, link-local, unique-local or carrier-grade NAT destination is refused unless the
  admin-only `egress_allow` setting names it; a cloud metadata address is refused even when named.
- The check runs on the **address actually dialed**, after resolution, so a name that resolves
  somewhere else at send time is judged where it goes. A zoned IPv6 address is refused; one that embeds
  an IPv4 destination (NAT64, 6to4, Teredo) is judged as that destination.
- https always, plain http only to a destination the allow list names; TLS verified; a redirect is the
  receiver's answer, never followed; every phase bounded; no proxy from the environment.
- A failed send's reason keeps a bounded excerpt of the answer, scrubbed of the URL and every credential.

## The heartbeat: what an alarm inside the platform cannot see

An alarm is raised by the thing it reports on, so the failures it can never report are those that stop
the server, its database or its network at once: silence reads as health. `rule-sweep-stalled` covers
the narrow case of a stalled sweep on a live server. For the rest there is a **dead-man's switch**: a
signal whose absence is the alarm, watched from outside.

Name a webhook [target](#notification-targets-where-a-message-goes) in the `heartbeat.target` setting
and the server sends it a signed request every `heartbeat.interval_seconds` (60 by default, 10 to
86400). It is built and signed by the notification pipeline (a target with no `hmac-key` bound is
refused, never sent unsigned), sent through the egress contract, and audited. The body carries the
server's name, its build and the count of open alarms on the platform's own components. It is off by
default. Omniglass does not host the receiver: point it at any service that pages when a ping fails to
arrive within a grace period of a few intervals.

## Cycle safety in the action layer

The collection, sample and alarm core is acyclic by construction, and only data authors events: an
event rule over samples is the only way a derived event enters the log. The loop left is the
**data-mediated control loop**: an action commands a device, the new state arrives as a sample, opens an
alarm, and fires the action again. Three rules close it:

1. **A rule never routes on its own consequences.** Every consequence write is labeled
   `provenance='calculated'` with its producer named (the health rollup's `property` row is
   `source_rule='health-rollup'`), and a calc rule's write never marks the rule that produced it
   ([ADR-0069](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0069-cycle-safety-is-provenance-based-not-topology-based)).
2. **Operator acts do not match action rules.** An action rule fires on `raised` and `cleared` alone;
   acknowledging and snoozing queue nothing. The one exception, a snooze's end re-announcing a raised
   alarm, re-sends a transition already announced and happens once, so it cannot loop.
3. **The control loop is lineage-guarded at dispatch**, needed once an action can run a command.

:::design[Flows and the lineage guard, tracked in #1344]
**Flows.** A multi-step action is a **flow**: a bounded list of `notify`, `wait`, `branch` and `command`
steps, started per open alarm by a transition, cancelled when the alarm clears, and finite by a step cap.
Single-step and multi-step are one model, distinguished only by the length of the step list, so the
built `action_rule` with its one `notify` step is the degenerate case. The canonical flow is an
escalation: notify, wait, re-check the real sample the alarm is built on, escalate if unchanged. A flow
carries its state in an `action` row (`status`, `current_step`, a due time), its step transitions on the
alarm's timeline. A flow does not author events: any effect returns as ordinary data.

**The lineage guard.** Before an action runs a command, the engine walks the triggering event's causation
lineage (`correlation_id` and `source_event_id` on the event, stamped by the command onto the value it
expects); if the same action and owner appear upstream it is suppressed, with a depth bound as backstop.
:::

:::design[Storm and dependency suppression, tracked in #1345]
Two primitives keep a fan-out from becoming a page storm. **Dependency suppression** mutes a child alarm
whose parent on the owner tree is already in `outage`. **Action-level grouping** coalesces alarms
sharing an owner, a tag or a `correlation_id` into one dispatch (one ticket with N members), the alarms
staying distinct rows. A switch reboot that takes down twenty endpoints then reads as its cause, not
twenty symptoms. Snooze is the primitive this consumes.
:::

## Namespacing

Event rules, severity levels, notification types and action rules carry the catalogs' `official` flag,
seeded through each catalog's authoritative upsert: an official row ships vetted and is read-only, and an
operator's row sits beside it. An official row never takes over an operator's; a clash stops the boot
naming it. The shipped notification types are `alarm-raised` and `alarm-cleared`, and the shipped action
rule is the platform routing above.
