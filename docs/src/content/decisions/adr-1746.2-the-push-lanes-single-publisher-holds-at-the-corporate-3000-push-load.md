---
title: "The push lane's single publisher holds at the corporate 3,000 push load"
date: 2026-11-11
status: "Accepted"
summary: "Accepted: measured on the corporate scenario with a booking service and a meeting-platform webhook account pushing at every site, the server-wide publish turn refused no push at 1,000 or 3,000 systems, its per-minute p99 wait stayed at or under 0.0125 ms and its worst wait was 2.18 ms, against the 5 second bound. The single publisher stays; the per-principal publisher is not built until a measurement shows the turn near its bound."
refs: [1746, 1696, 1743, 1758, 1623]

- **Date:** 2026-11-11 | **Status:** Accepted | **Pages:** [messaging](/architecture/messaging/#accounting-every-item-the-loss-ratio), [lab](/guides/lab/#reading-the-push-lanes-turn)
- **Decision:** (#1746, under #1623, settling the thin cut of [ADR-1696](#adr-1696-the-push-lanes-batches-carry-a-ledger-minted-by-the-server-and-read-as-their-own-ingest-row) that serializes publishes server-wide.)
  1. **One publisher per server stays.** The push lane keeps its one turn: one batch in flight to
     the stream at a time, one boot per server process, a dense seq in the stream's order.
  2. **The evidence.** The corporate scenario (#1743's pushers: a booking service and a meeting
     platform webhook account at every site, each every 30 seconds), a 3 minute warm-up and a
     10 minute window per size, read from the `push-lane-*` figures (ADR-1746) and the publish row:

     | | 1,000 systems | 3,000 systems |
     |---|---|---|
     | Sites and pushers | 18 sites, 36 pushers | 30 sites, 60 pushers |
     | Pushes in the window | 1,800 (3 a second) | 3,001 (5 a second) |
     | p99 turn wait, per minute | 0.005 to 0.0125 ms | 0.004 to 0.0105 ms |
     | Worst turn wait | 0.035 ms | 2.18 ms |
     | Refused at the turn | 0 | 0 |

     The worst p99 is about 1/400,000 of the 5 second bound and the worst single wait 1/2,300 of
     it. A push almost never finds the turn held, so the turn is not the push lane's limit at the
     corporate target.
  3. **What would reopen it.** A run whose `push-lane-turn-wait-p99` climbs toward the bound, or any
     `refused-turn` on the publish row, at a size inside the target. Then the change is the one
     #1746 scoped: a publisher per pushing principal, so one pusher's batches stay ordered and
     different pushers publish in parallel, each with its own boot.
- **Context:** ADR-1696 serialized the push lane's publishes so its seq is dense and its resolved
  is always the seq below, and accepted the cap as a thin cut because the stream is embedded. #1743
  put pushers at every site of the corporate lab, so a 3,000 run could have queued pushes behind
  one another until some were refused, which the loss gate would read as the push lane's loss.
  ADR-1746 made the turn measurable; this reads the measurement.
- **Thin cuts:**
  - At 3,000 the delivery read did not settle (#1758): the consumer always held one batch, so the
    node lane's hops, the push lane's ingest rows and end to end read `unsettled`. The ruling does
    not rest on them. The publish row and the turn figures come from the server's metrics, not the
    receipts, and both read.
  - The turn wait does not show how long a turn is held, so the headroom above 5 pushes a second is
    not measured, only that the turn is almost always free at that rate.
  - Each size ran once.
- **Not chosen:**
  - **A publisher per pushing principal now.** It splits the push lane into a boot per principal
    and a ledger read per boot, for a queue that measured empty. It stays the named change if the
    turn ever nears its bound.
  - **Raising the 5 second bound.** Nothing came near it.
