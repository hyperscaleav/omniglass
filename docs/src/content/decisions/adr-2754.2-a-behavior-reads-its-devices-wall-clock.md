title: "A behavior reads its device's wall clock and draws from its device's source"
date: 2026-11-26
status: Accepted
summary: "A simulator behavior's templates read the time through the device's own clock (now, and nextSlot for the next boundary of a slot), which a test pins with SetClock, and draw integers and chances (randInt, chance) from the device's seeded source; a schedule that sits on the wall clock is therefore deterministic per device and testable over a simulated day."

- **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Accepted | **Pages:** [simulation](/architecture/simulation/)
- **Decision:** The behavior FuncMap gains `now` (the device clock's unix seconds), `nextSlot t m` (the
  next boundary of an m-minute slot strictly after t, counted from the epoch, so a 30-minute slot falls
  on the hour and the half hour), `randInt lo hi` and `chance p`. The clock is the device's own (the one
  its computed fields read, `Device.SetClock`), never `time.Now` read directly, and the draws come from
  the per-device source seeded from its id. A route's templates keep the process clock (amended by ADR-2800: a route's and a command's `now` read the device clock too).
- **Context:** The architect ruled (#2754) that a video bar talks on a realistic meeting schedule, not a
  call every 90 seconds: meetings on 30 or 60 minute boundaries (sometimes 15), starting a little early
  or late, the room booked about half its slots, because a 90-second cycle on every bar costs the
  3,000-system goal for no realism. A schedule needs the hour, and a test needs to hold the hour still
  to drive a day in a moment; the device clock was already the one seam a test pins. Booking tables do
  not exist yet; when they do, a room's bookings would drive the same schedule instead of its draws.
