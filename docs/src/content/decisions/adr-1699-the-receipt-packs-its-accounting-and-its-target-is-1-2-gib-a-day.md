- **Date:** 2026-11-12 | **Status:** Accepted | **Pages:** [scaling](/architecture/scaling/#ingest-one-batch-per-tick)
- **Decision:** (#1699; revises the receipts-a-day row of the scaling page's ingest table.) The receipt stores its ledger as `bigint[]`, a (count, before) pair per kind slot, and its outcome as `int[]`, sparse (code, n) pairs. The kind slots and outcome codes are append-only lists in `internal/delivery`, pinned by tests, and a migration converts the jsonb rows in place. A count past its column's range is saturated at the ceiling rather than refusing the batch: a node's ledger can declare any uint64, and its shortfall still reads as loss. The target for corporate at 1,000 systems becomes **receipts a day under 1.2 GiB**, about 800 B a batch at the 18 batches a second the table expects.
- **Consequences:** On a three-system corporate lab (`lab measure --systems 3`, one 4-minute window), the packed receipt reads 151 MiB a day at 2.74 batches a second, down from 219 MiB at 2.82: about 669 B a batch, from about 942. WAL a sample is 486 B, from 510. Loss is 0 on every kind and hop in both runs. At 18 batches a second that is about 0.97 GiB a day, from about 1.36. Receipts are pruned at a day, so this is the table's standing size, about 1% of the 81 GiB a day of metric table the same fleet writes. `TestReceiptStaysInItsBudget` holds a ledgered receipt's row under 330 B; it measures 303 B, from 525 B as jsonb, and 516 B a receipt on disk with both indexes, from 770 B.
- **Context:** The 0.5 GiB target was set for the narrow receipt, about 290 B a batch, before every batch carried its ledger and outcome ([ADR-1641](#adr-1641-every-telemetry-item-is-accounted-on-its-batchs-receipt-and-read-as-a-loss-ratio)). 0.5 GiB a day at 18 batches a second is about 345 B a batch. The packed receipt compacted by `VACUUM FULL` still holds about 475 B (heap 319, primary key 106, the `landed_at` index 40), so only dropping the accounting reaches the old target, and the accounting is how the loss ratio is read.
- **Not chosen:**
  - **Insert the receipt last, with its outcome.** It saves the dead tuple the outcome update leaves, about 66 B a batch, but the receipt is inserted first because its key is the exactly-once claim: the transaction that loses the race stops before it writes a sample ([ADR-0311](#adr-0311-a-telemetry-batch-lands-once-in-one-transaction-under-its-receipt)). Moving the claim to the end trades a fixed width for a change to the redelivery path.
  - **Narrow the batch id or drop an index.** The batch id is the subject and a UUIDv7, the key a redelivery collides on; the `landed_at` index serves the prune and the receipts read. Each saves tens of bytes against a correctness or a read path.
  - **Shorten the 24-hour retention.** Out of scope for #1699, and the day is what the closed loop and the receipts read look back over.
