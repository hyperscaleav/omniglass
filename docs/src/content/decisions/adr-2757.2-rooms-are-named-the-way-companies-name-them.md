- **Date:** 2026-11-27 | **Status:** Accepted | **Pages:** [simulation](/architecture/simulation/), [lab](/developer/lab/)
- **Decision:** A real building has both a name a company gave a room ("Curie", "Huddle 3") and the
  address of the place it sits ("Hq Tower 12 01"). The scenario plan labels each system with the name,
  drawn from one theme per site (`room_names` on the archetype, word lists held in
  `internal/lab/scenario/roomnames.go` and nowhere else) or, where a standard declares a `room_name`,
  from what the room is, numbered within its site; the location stays the address. A name is a pure
  function of the scenario, the site, the standard and the room's index, and unique within its site.
  The world carries the label with each room, so the meeting and scheduling platform simulators list a
  room by its name with the address beside it, and the console names a system with its address
  wherever it is listed (the system page's title, a device's Systems it serves, a conduit's bindings
  and picker, search, Explore and the system blade), the address left out where it would repeat the
  name. `fleet.NameGenerator` (the adjective-surname generator) is retired: nothing else used it.
- **Context:** The lab labelled systems "Boring Edison" while the location tree carried the address,
  and a sole system's page was titled by its location alone (#872), so a lab's rooms read as an
  address on one surface and an unrelated name on the next. The architect ruled that reality is both:
  companies name their rooms. This diverges from [ADR-0914](/architecture/decisions/#adr-0914-the-simulator-catalog-carries-no-fleet-or-room-archetype-vocabulary),
  which kept the generator for the scale lab's resolver.
